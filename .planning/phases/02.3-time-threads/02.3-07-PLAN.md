<objective>
**As a** person looking back at something I wrote over weeks, **I want to** fly along the whole thread, zoom from the entire span down to single dots, and click any moment to read the document exactly as it stood then, **so that** the history is somewhere I can travel rather than a file I can only scroll.

This plan turns the stage sideways: the whole thread left to right, honest about every gap, navigable by wheel, keyboard, session and date — and a read-only past stage above it.

Purpose: D-15 through D-18 are the read-back half of the feature. The constraint that shapes all of it is honesty about time: the side view never compresses a gap and never reshuffles the line, which is exactly what makes "huge gaps broken up with small planets" the right way to navigate it.

Output: the side camera, the navigation model, the date scrubber, and document-at-a-moment.
</objective>

    Gravity is the part most easily got wrong, because it must never fight a person who is simply moving the pointer across the screen. It acts **only** when all of these hold: the pointer is within 48px of a session pill, pointer speed is below 120px/s, and no wheel or drag happened in the last 150ms. It is a critically damped spring on the view centre with strength `k0 · clamp(log2(1 + letters)/10, 0.2, 1)`, so bigger sessions pull harder. Any movement above 240px/s, any key press, or the settings toggle cancels it immediately. There is no pull while moving freely — that is the difference between "small planets you can fall toward" and a view that snatches at the cursor.
